# Assessment Library

## Purpose

The assessment library is a structured collection of questions, interactive tasks, exams, and generators connected to both the mathematical and thinking competency graphs.

Assessment must determine not only whether an answer is correct, but whether the learner understands the structure, can decompose an unfamiliar problem, can reconstruct the solution, and can transfer the principle into another form or context.

## Three assessment outcomes

```text
Content mastery
Understands the mathematical concept.

Thinking mastery
Can decompose, represent, reason, select strategies, and verify.

Transfer mastery
Can apply the same structure or principle in a different or real-life context.
```

These outcomes should be tracked separately.

## Assessment modes

- **Diagnostic:** locate the learner’s actual mathematical and decomposition starting point.
- **Prerequisite check:** determine which earlier skill blocks a target skill.
- **Guided practice:** teach and assess within the same interaction.
- **Decomposition check:** evaluate goal identification, known/unknown separation, subproblem creation, ordering, reconstruction, and verification.
- **Mastery check:** confirm independent performance through varied evidence.
- **Learning-integrity check:** distinguish structural understanding from familiar-layout recognition, guessing, or answer memory.
- **Retention check:** verify knowledge after time has passed.
- **Representation transfer:** verify the same idea across objects, diagrams, number lines, symbols, tables, or words.
- **Structural transfer:** verify use in a new story, format, or mathematical domain.
- **Life-transfer reflection:** verify that the learner can connect a thinking principle to a relevant non-mathematical problem.
- **Grade readiness:** measure readiness for the next Bulgarian grade.
- **NVO preparation:** match official reasoning demands and answer formats.
- **Challenge mode:** use olympiad-style reasoning for ready learners.

## Surface-pattern protection

A learner may answer familiar-looking tasks quickly without understanding the underlying relationship.

Important skill checks should vary:

- numbers;
- wording;
- layout;
- colors and visual arrangement;
- answer positions;
- representation;
- context;
- problem direction;
- time between attempts.

The system should compare familiar-format and novel-format performance.

```text
High familiar-format accuracy
+ Low novel-format accuracy
= likely surface dependence
```

This should trigger additional teaching and structural variation, not punishment or shame.

## Evidence methods

### Surface variation

Keep the mathematical relationship while changing presentation details.

### Representation variation

Move between equation, objects, diagram, number line, table, word problem, and construction.

### Error diagnosis

Ask the learner to locate the first invalid step and select why it fails.

### Construction

Ask the learner to create an example, counterexample, representation, decomposition, or solution plan.

### Step selection and ordering

Ask which action should happen first and how later actions should be ordered.

### Reconstruction

After solving components, ask how they combine to answer the original question.

### Explanation selection

Use carefully designed reasoning choices so the learner can show why the answer works without requiring long free-text explanations every time.

### Delayed retention

Return to the idea after time has passed and without the previous surrounding context.

### Life transfer

After a meaningful mathematical success, ask the learner to identify where the same principle could help in a project, plan, decision, or other relevant situation.

## Decomposition evidence

The assessment system should observe whether the learner can:

- identify the main goal;
- distinguish relevant from irrelevant information;
- separate knowns and unknowns;
- create useful subproblems;
- choose the next useful action;
- order the steps;
- solve individual parts;
- reconstruct the final result;
- verify the final result;
- complete the process independently.

The amount of decomposition support required should be part of the learner state.

## Item quality

Every item or template should eventually declare:

- target mathematical skill;
- target thinking competencies;
- prerequisites;
- assessment purpose;
- difficulty;
- familiarity level;
- representation;
- answer format;
- correct answer;
- deterministic validation method;
- misconceptions tested;
- surface-recognition risks;
- support available;
- decomposition level;
- widget type;
- expected content-mastery evidence;
- expected thinking-mastery evidence;
- expected transfer evidence;
- source and licence;
- reviewer status.

## Source policy

The final bank combines:

1. original, teacher-reviewed templates;
2. deterministic parameterized generators;
3. openly licensed material where commercial reuse is allowed;
4. official Bulgarian exams as calibration and reference;
5. licensed publisher content only under explicit agreement.

Grade 4 and grade 7 NVO are central calibration sources. Grades 5, 6, and 8 require original entry, unit, end-of-grade, readiness, thinking, and transfer assessments.

Later grade 9–12 research should identify additional assessment patterns for proof, functions, modelling, statistics, probability, trigonometry, optimization, and advanced abstraction.

## Assessment principle

A correct answer is evidence, not proof of mastery.

Consider:

- independence;
- hints used;
- decomposition support;
- response time;
- answer changes;
- confidence;
- representation;
- success on varied examples;
- novel-format performance;
- delayed retention;
- reconstruction;
- mathematical transfer;
- life transfer.

The strongest progress signal is not the number of completed questions. It is how much less help the learner needs to understand, decompose, solve, reconstruct, and verify an unfamiliar problem.
