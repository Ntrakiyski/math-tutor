# Bulgarian Mathematics and Thinking Competency Graphs

## Goal

Build two connected competency graphs:

1. a mathematical graph aligned initially with the Bulgarian education system for grades 4–8;
2. a thinking-competency graph extending from grade 4 through grade 12.

The graphs must support a learner who may be enrolled in a higher grade while missing foundations from earlier grades.

## Curriculum layers

### 1. Bulgarian expected outcomes

Official Bulgarian Ministry of Education programs define what is expected in each grade, official terminology, topic boundaries, competencies, and learning outcomes.

### 2. Foundational prerequisite graph

Marble Skill Taxonomy and reviewed extensions help decompose broad curriculum outcomes into smaller prerequisites.

### 3. Grade 4–8 mathematical bridge

The key mathematical asset is an independently authored graph connecting:

```text
Foundational number sense
→ arithmetic
→ fractions and decimals
→ ratios and percentages
→ rational numbers
→ elementary algebra
→ geometry and measurement
→ grade 7 and grade 8 readiness
```

### 4. Grade 4–12 thinking curriculum

Each mathematical topic should also connect to transferable thinking principles such as:

- decomposition;
- identifying knowns and unknowns;
- next-action selection;
- abstraction;
- representation switching;
- structural pattern recognition;
- estimation and sense checking;
- assumptions and constraints;
- reversibility;
- evidence and proof;
- counterexamples;
- systematic enumeration;
- optimization and trade-offs;
- proportional thinking;
- reasoning under uncertainty;
- local and global thinking;
- invariants;
- iteration and correction;
- precision;
- reconstruction.

### 5. School and exam alignment

Connect competencies to:

- grade;
- official outcome;
- NVO relevance;
- textbook sequence;
- assessment type;
- common misconceptions;
- surface-recognition risks;
- suitable widgets;
- Manim explanation templates;
- thinking competencies;
- life-transfer opportunities.

## Competency record direction

Each mathematical skill should eventually include:

- stable identifier;
- Bulgarian name;
- plain-language description;
- expected grade;
- mathematical domain;
- hard and soft prerequisites;
- evidence of content mastery;
- evidence of thinking mastery;
- evidence of transfer mastery;
- misconception patterns;
- anti-patterns such as surface matching or keyword-based operation choice;
- diagnostic question families;
- teaching widget families;
- decomposition support level;
- animation families;
- thinking competencies trained;
- life-transfer examples;
- official source references;
- review status.

## Grade roles

### Grade 4

Elementary arithmetic, place value, measurement, geometry, problem solving, NVO readiness, known/unknown separation, sequencing, and basic verification.

### Grade 5

Deeper fractions, decimals, measurement, geometry, structured problems, representation switching, proportional foundations, and decomposition of multi-step tasks.

### Grade 6

Rational-number thinking, proportional relationships, geometry, algebraic preparation, assumptions, constraints, estimation, and applied modelling.

### Grade 7

Rational numbers, algebraic reasoning, equations, geometry, modelling, NVO preparation, reconstruction, transfer, and validation of unfamiliar forms.

### Grade 8

More formal algebra, functions and graphs, geometry, reasoning, abstraction, relationship modelling, and high-school readiness.

### Grades 9–12 thinking horizon

Later high-school topics should extend the thinking graph through:

- formal proof;
- functions and change;
- trigonometric modelling;
- statistics and evidence;
- probability and uncertainty;
- optimization;
- advanced abstraction;
- local versus global behavior;
- modelling assumptions and limitations;
- calculus concepts where applicable.

Detailed Bulgarian mathematical mapping for grades 9–12 remains future scope, but the thinking principles should be researched while building the earlier curriculum so the system grows coherently.

## Remediation rule

Grade enrollment does not control the starting point.

The system finds the lowest important missing prerequisite and constructs a dignified recovery path toward the current grade expectation.

The same rule applies to thinking skills: a learner may know the content but still need support identifying the goal, creating subproblems, selecting the next action, or reconstructing the result.
