# Learning-Centered Gamification

## Purpose

Gamification should strengthen learning, confidence, persistence, and independence. It must not create empty engagement, shame, or unhealthy pressure.

## Reward

Reward:

- trying again;
- asking for a useful hint;
- correcting a misconception;
- remembering after time has passed;
- solving independently;
- explaining reasoning;
- using a second representation;
- completing a suitable challenge;
- returning after a difficult session.

## Avoid over-rewarding

Do not over-reward speed, repetitive easy questions, random clicking, excessively long sessions, or streak maintenance at any cost.

## Progress should represent

- new understanding;
- strengthened retention;
- reduced support;
- increased confidence;
- greater transfer;
- recovery of a prerequisite;
- movement toward grade expectations.

## Emotional safety

Never compare the learner negatively with classmates, expose a humiliating grade label, punish lost streaks, frame easier work as failure, or praise speed over thinking.

## Possible metaphors

- rebuilding a mathematical map;
- restoring paths between skills;
- growing a constellation of connected ideas;
- collecting mathematical methods;
- strengthening foundations that support future areas.

Mistakes should reveal the next useful step.
